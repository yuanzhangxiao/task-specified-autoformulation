# Hybrid variable and pre-function topology readiness

The versioned `scientific-staged-prefunction-hybrid-2` campaign reruns the same
two reviewed Phase-B public cells and three seeds as the v1 integration. It
still stops before functional expressions, initialization, fitting, scientific
judging, test access, or private-reference access.

Level 0 deterministically registers external inputs, covariates, time, and
public supplied sources required by reviewed mechanisms or target-composition
contracts. Target representation and internal scientific variables remain
proposer decisions.

Each variable response is a batch, but runtime validation is item-local after
provider-schema validation. Safe identifiers, compatible public roles,
unchanged accepted definitions, and construction limits determine whether one
entry is retained. An invalid entry does not discard valid siblings. The next
bounded call sees only rejected entries and unresolved typed obligations.
Resolved mechanism or target agenda items are skipped.

A reviewed dynamic-memory requirement creates a typed obligation. During its
variable call, the proposer must introduce or explicitly reuse a differential
mediator distinct from public targets and drivers. Equation requests expose the
associated driver-to-memory-to-target obligation. Memory equations are built
before public target equations, and a missing local driver or mediator path
activates the ordinary bounded equation repair for only that left-hand side.

Scientific interaction signs remain proposer-owned unless the public task gives
an exact direct sign. The runtime verifies those explicit signs and otherwise
preserves positive, negative, or unrestricted choices. It reports the choice
distribution but does not use abstention as a readiness gate.

The campaign reports topology completion over all six tasks. Target, source,
and necessary topology-obligation coverage are separately reported conditional
on completed topologies. “Topology obligation” deliberately does not claim to
verify nonlinear functional semantics that do not exist before the next stage.
