# What the latent-dynamics experiments identify

A specification can constrain a *realization* while leaving its coordinates undetermined. For example, take a state with `dx/dt = -a*x + b*u` and output `y = c*x`. For any positive scale s, the coordinate `z = s*x` obeys `dz/dt = -a*z + s*b*u` and gives exactly the same output with readout `c*z/s` and scaled initialization. Even perfect measurements of that output under every tested input cannot identify the numerical scale of the hidden state. More general changes of coordinates can also preserve behavior. Physiological state labels alone do not resolve these ambiguities.

The available evidence supports three different questions. Observational fit measures whether a frozen model generates the measured targets from permitted initial information and supplied inputs. Specification checks test declared requirements on the executed model. Intervention comparisons measure the change in generated trajectories against independently generated paired physical conditions. On T2-hard, a delayed fall in plasma glucose can follow increased disposal or suppressed glucose production. The public balance check leaves this ambiguity unresolved. The simulator-facing assay measures disposal and production separately when a verified physical readout is available. Train-calibrated single-coordinate readouts test decodability under the declared decoder class. An unsuccessful decoder can also reflect distributed latent coordinates.

Classical latent identification results impose assumptions on the observation map, source distributions, and the variation that distinguishes sources. Time-contrastive learning uses temporal nonstationarity. The stationary time-series formulation exploits suitable dependence structure and an invertible mixing map. Auxiliary-variable and identifiable-VAE models constrain conditional source distributions and their variation with an observed auxiliary variable. The present controlled ODE search does not establish those hypotheses. Its evidence concerns specific responses and representations in these experiments. [Hyvärinen and Morioka, 2016](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2016/hash/d305281faf947ca7acade9ad5c8c818c-Abstract.html) · [Hyvärinen and Morioka, 2017](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v54/hyvarinen17a.html) · [Hyvärinen, Sasaki and Turner, 2019](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v89/hyvarinen19a.html) · [Khemakhem et al., 2020](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v108/khemakhem20a.html)

SINDy autoencoders jointly learn coordinates and sparse dynamics. Delay autoencoders use measurement histories to learn a dynamical representation under partial observation. These approaches establish important precedents for joint representation and equation learning. Here the search also receives user-supplied mechanism obligations and must return executable equations and causal initializers. Whether two proposed models agree on the observed channels, implement those obligations, and extrapolate to the same interventions can be tested independently. [Champion et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.02107) · [Bakarji et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.05136)

Structured nonlinear ICA also gives identification results in the presence of additive measurement noise under assumptions on the latent process and its dependence. Those assumptions would need independent scientific justification before applying such a result to a controlled, partially observed simulator. [Hälvä et al., 2021](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/hash/0cdbb4e65815fbaf79689b15482e7575-Abstract.html)
