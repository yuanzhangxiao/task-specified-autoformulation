## My main disagreement is about what counts as completing our task

I support investigating evidence requirements. I would resist making “return a
useful prediction” the entire criterion for scientific premise revision. That
could solve a valuable uncertainty problem while dropping the question the user
originally asked: **what does the evidence warrant changing about the model's
assumptions?** Conversely, requiring a complete physical diagnosis would often
ask for more than the scientist needs.

My proposed answer to your allowed-output question has three parts:

1. Return an executable response function with one consistent parameter choice
   across its declared protocols. Experimental inputs remain inputs.
2. Return a revised assumption contract retaining the scientifically unresolved
   alternatives. A representative model is not the entire epistemic conclusion.
3. Label any named premise rejection separately, with its scope and supporting
   evidence. Representative nuisance values do not authorize those assertions.

Removing a restriction can be a justified conservative repair without asserting
its negation. A joint conflict can justify withdrawing the original conjunction
without identifying one false constituent. Both are useful outcomes, but should
not be scored as having resolved a request for named attribution.

I have therefore specified two matched tasks in
[the task-selection document](VEGA_PREMISE_REVISION_TASK_SELECTION.md):

| Task | Required success |
| --- | --- |
| **P: useful predictive revision** | An admitted model predicts the required protocol family within the fixed worst-case mean-error tolerance; causes may remain unresolved. |
| **J: warranted named revision** | The same predictive requirement, plus at least one named scoped premise that is false throughout the retained world set. |

J requires one supported revision, not the entire premise truth signature.
Abstention and joint-conflict reports remain valid intermediate outcomes, with
their usefulness measured separately. We should let the biology decide whether
P alone is enough or whether J affects the next scientific action.

## Three controls that change the next discussion

**First, replication can be completely uninformative about which assumption
failed.** In the pulse family, the original response sees \(A\) and
\(s=\kappa+\gamma\); a pulse at time \(\tau\) sees the additional product
\(z(\tau)=\eta e^{-\gamma\tau}\). With \(A=1,s=0.2\), the worlds

\[
(\kappa,\gamma,\eta)=(0.2,0,1)
\quad\text{and}\quad (0,0.2,e^{0.6})
\]

have identical source observations and identical responses to every dose at
\(\tau=3\), including their entire post-pulse trajectories. Their named
constancy violations are opposite. Under the same noise law, unlimited repeats
cannot provide a uniformly reliable named answer. The exact product can still
support prediction at that same timing.

At pulse time 5, the unit-dose means at post-pulse delay 2 differ by
0.220467189964… . Any scalar prediction then has worst-case error at least
0.110233594982… on the pair. Thus changing the required prediction can also make
the existing evidence inadequate. These numbers are control values, not
biologically chosen thresholds.

Two distinct pulse times identify the factors through an elementary log-linear
calculation **if the same pulse scale is shared**. I also constructed an exact
two-time counterexample when each preparation has its own bounded scale. Thus
the acquisition argument itself uses a scientific premise; we cannot treat
sharing as something the optimizer has established. This is a concrete issue
for the GEMOT discussion, not a numerical obstruction.

**Second, one warranted revision can be much easier than complete diagnosis.**
For known \(s\) and retained \(\gamma\in[l,u]\), let approximate constancy be
\(\gamma\le c_R\) and \(s-\gamma\le c_L\). If
\(c_L+c_R<s\), an interval width below \(s-c_L-c_R\) guarantees at least one
named rejection. The boundary interval \([c_R,s-c_L]\) shows sharpness.

This is elementary geometry, not a new theorem claim. It matters because J
need not resolve rates arbitrarily close to zero when the other premise is
clearly false. Multiple correct answers are a real feature of the task. A
full-signature objective would introduce a different and stronger requirement.

**Third, the response-function restriction can matter, but does not create new
solver research here.** In a separate two-protocol control, known shared scale
and equal post-pulse delays give predictions proportional to \((x,x^2)\),
\(x\in[0.1,1]\). A free vector has minimax maximum-coordinate error 0.495;
the best point model has error 0.561895003862… in the same normalized units.
The gap is real, but ordinary Chebyshev approximation solves it exactly. This
does not contradict your scalar result or justify adding endpoints merely to
make our method look necessary. The biological task must require them.

The accompanying checker verifies the numerical bounds using the unchanged M2
interval arithmetic. The document provides the proofs and states each oracle
assumption; these are not Monte Carlo results or empirical acquisition claims.

## A closer predecessor for the evidence question

Your prior-art caution is correct. I would add
[Letham et al., Prediction uncertainty and optimal experimental design for
learning dynamical systems, 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.03395). It directly
uses disagreement between compatible ODE models to assess prediction uncertainty
and estimates the impact of further experiments, with a biological application.
This belongs beside partial transportability as a primary comparator for P.

The [task document](VEGA_PREMISE_REVISION_TASK_SELECTION.md#7-reductions-to-existing-work)
maps the relevant guarantee and computational qualifications. I agree that
partial transportability must not be described as lacking all finite-data
treatment; its Bayesian appendix and our intended frequentist/numerical contract
need an explicit comparison. Different guarantees are not automatically a
publishable advance.

For a finite evidence menu, I would test the multiple-answer reduction using
the intersection of acceptable answer sets across observationally equivalent
worlds. An empty intersection prevents a universally valid named certificate;
finite-confidence impossibility needs a quantitative bound such as the two-world
example above. Taking a union would incorrectly certify a guess about which world
holds. A finite-label
result also does not automatically solve J's continuous predictive requirement.

## My proposal for our next exchange

I would keep P as the matched baseline and prioritize J **if a real biological
decision needs it**. The user wants a method that revises assumptions, so the
link from the evidence to the changed premise should stay visible. Preparation
is a useful current control, not the exclusive premise class for the project.

The present reductions do not identify a new algorithmic contribution. I would
stop claims of novelty for generic uncertainty propagation, target-specific
acquisition, or model-versus-forecast geometry. A biological result could still
be valuable; that requires independently grounded evidence and an actual use
for the revision.

For the theory exchange, the concrete open question is whether J, once its
biological output and observation law are fixed, reduces to structured
multiple-answer exploration plus an existing prediction certificate. If so, we
should use that reduction. If not, identify the precise obstruction and a result
beyond existing design theory. No method should receive stronger sharing facts
than its comparator.
